# Security Policy

This is the full vulnerability disclosure policy for Apiloom Gateway CE. The short version lives at the repository root in [SECURITY.md](../SECURITY.md); the long version lives here.

If you believe you have found a vulnerability, please report it as described in [Reporting](#reporting). Do not open a public GitHub issue for a vulnerability under any circumstances.

## Scope

This policy covers the components of the Apiloom Gateway CE project, including:

- The data plane (`cmd/gateway`, `internal/gateway`, `internal/block/...`).
- The control plane (`cmd/controlplane`, `internal/controlplane`, `internal/config`).
- The CLI (`cmd/fgctl`).
- The plugin SDK (`pkg/flex`).
- The Helm chart (`charts/`).
- The official container images published to `ghcr.io/mindfulcto-labs/apiloom-ce/*`.
- The example plugins in `examples/plugins/` (security issues that could affect users following the examples).
- The docs site at `docs.apiloom.io` and the marketing site at `apiloom.io`, *only* for issues that could compromise visitors (e.g., XSS, supply-chain). Unauthenticated content errors and typos are not in scope.

The following are **out of scope**:

- The Apiloom Platform (enterprise product) — please contact `security@apiloom.io` separately and we will route appropriately.
- Findings that require physical access to a user's machine.
- Findings that require the user to have already been compromised (e.g., "if an attacker has root").
- Denial of service via volumetric traffic against public infrastructure.
- Findings in third-party dependencies that have not yet been fixed upstream, unless the project's use of the dependency is the actual root cause.
- Self-XSS or social-engineering scenarios.
- Missing security headers on pages with no sensitive content.
- Reports generated by automated scanners with no demonstrated impact.

## Reporting

Send vulnerability reports to **`security@apiloom.io`**.

For sensitive disclosures, encrypt with our PGP key:

- **Fingerprint:** to be published. Until then, contact `security@apiloom.io` to arrange a secure channel.
- **Key URL:** `https://apiloom.io/.well-known/security/pgp` (planned).
- **`security.txt`:** `https://apiloom.io/.well-known/security.txt` (planned, [RFC 9116](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9116)).

Include in your report:

- A clear description of the vulnerability.
- The affected component and version (`apiloom-gateway --version`, image digest, or commit SHA).
- A minimal reproduction. A failing test, a `curl` command, or a short script is ideal.
- Your assessment of impact (what an attacker can do, against what assets).
- Whether you have shared the finding with anyone else.
- How you would like to be credited in the public advisory, or whether you prefer to remain anonymous.

We will accept reports in English. Other languages are welcome but may slow our response.

## What happens after you report

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Within **24 hours** | We acknowledge receipt of your report. A real human will respond. |
| Within **72 hours** | We confirm whether we are accepting the report as in scope and assign initial severity. |
| Within **7 days** | We share a triage summary: confirmed/disputed, assigned severity, owner on our side, and an initial fix timeline. |
| Ongoing | We keep you in the loop. You can ask for an update at any time. |
| At fix | We coordinate a release window with you, including any embargoed disclosure. |
| At disclosure | We publish a [GitHub Security Advisory](https://github.com/mindfulcto-labs/apiloom-ce/security/advisories), credit you (unless you opt out), and request a CVE. |

If you have not heard from us within 24 hours, please send a follow-up to `security@apiloom.io`. Inboxes get full and we would rather hear twice than miss your report.

## Severity classification

We use [CVSS v3.1](https://www.first.org/cvss/v3-1/specification-document) for vulnerability scoring. The CVSS base score determines the severity tier and the response SLAs below. The maintainers may adjust the published severity if real-world exploitability differs significantly from the base score; the rationale is included in the advisory.

| Severity | CVSS v3.1 base | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Critical** | 9.0 – 10.0 | Unauthenticated RCE on the gateway. Auth bypass that exposes other tenants' traffic. Pre-auth SQL injection in the control plane. |
| **High** | 7.0 – 8.9 | Authenticated privilege escalation across tenants. Forgeable signed tokens. Plugin sandbox escape. Persistent XSS on the docs site. |
| **Medium** | 4.0 – 6.9 | Information disclosure of non-sensitive metadata across tenant boundaries. Rate-limit bypass under specific config. CSRF on a low-impact control plane endpoint. |
| **Low** | 0.1 – 3.9 | Verbose error messages leaking stack traces. Logging of high-cardinality but non-sensitive data. Missing defense-in-depth headers. |

## Response SLAs

These are the timelines we commit to between **confirming a report as in scope** and **shipping a fix**. They are upper bounds; we move faster when we can.

| Severity | Patch target | Public disclosure target |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | **7 days** | Coordinated, no later than **14 days** after patch availability |
| High | **30 days** | Coordinated, no later than **30 days** after patch availability |
| Medium | **90 days** | At the next minor release |
| Low | Next minor release | At the next minor release |

If we cannot meet a target, we will tell you why and propose a new target. We will not silently slip.

We follow a **90-day disclosure deadline** from the date the report is confirmed. If we cannot ship a fix in that window, the vulnerability will be disclosed at day 90 with whatever mitigation guidance we have, regardless of fix status. We believe this is fair to users, who deserve to know about unfixed risks in software they are running.

## Supported versions

Security patches are produced for:

- The **current minor release** (`vX.Y.*`).
- The **previous minor release** (`vX.Y-1.*`), on a best-effort basis, for **90 days** after the next minor release ships.

Older minor releases do not receive patches. Users on older versions are expected to upgrade. We will help with migration questions in GitHub Discussions.

## Embargo and coordinated disclosure

For Critical and High issues we will work with you on a coordinated embargo. Default embargo length is **the patch SLA + 14 days**, to give downstream packagers (Helm chart users, OS package maintainers, image consumers) time to update. We are open to longer embargoes in exceptional cases (e.g., the issue requires upstream Lura framework changes).

During an embargo:

- The fix is developed in a private security advisory branch on GitHub.
- Patches are reviewed by the active Maintainers only.
- We notify a small list of pre-disclosed downstream consumers (currently: none; this list will grow as adopters appear and opt in via `security@apiloom.io`).
- We do **not** notify the press, analysts, or competitors.
- We do **not** publish performance benchmarks or marketing material that hint at the issue.

When the embargo lifts, we publish the advisory, the patched release, and a public-facing post-mortem if the issue warrants one.

## Safe harbor

We will not pursue civil action, criminal complaint, or report to law enforcement against security researchers who:

1. **Make a good-faith effort** to comply with this policy.
2. **Avoid privacy violations.** Do not access data beyond what is strictly necessary to demonstrate the vulnerability. Do not exfiltrate data. If you incidentally encounter user data, stop, delete it, and tell us.
3. **Avoid degradation of service.** Do not run high-volume or destructive testing against production infrastructure or shared services. Use your own deployment for testing where possible.
4. **Avoid social engineering** of Apiloom employees, contractors, or community members.
5. **Do not exploit the vulnerability** for any purpose beyond demonstrating its existence.
6. **Give us a reasonable opportunity** to respond and resolve the issue before any disclosure.

If you act in good faith under this policy, we authorize your testing of the components in scope and consider your activity to be authorized access for the purposes of the [Computer Fraud and Abuse Act](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1030), the [DMCA](https://www.copyright.gov/dmca/), and equivalent laws in other jurisdictions.

If your testing uncovers data that belongs to a third party (e.g., a user of an Apiloom adopter), please notify us immediately and stop testing. We will coordinate with the affected party.

This safe harbor language is informed by [disclose.io](https://disclose.io/) and the work of the [Bugcrowd disclosure team](https://www.bugcrowd.com/resources/initiatives/disclosure/). It is not a contract. It reflects our intent. If you have specific legal questions, please ask before testing.

## What we ask in return

- Give us a chance to fix the issue before disclosing it publicly.
- Do not test against infrastructure you do not own without our written permission.
- If you find a vulnerability while using a deployed Apiloom adopter's service, please report it to that adopter first; we are happy to help mediate.

## Recognition

We will credit reporters in the published advisory and in `CHANGELOG.md` for the patch release, unless you ask us not to. We do not currently run a paid bug bounty program. If that changes, we will publish the program details here. We are happy to provide letters of recommendation, public thanks, and (for substantial contributions) Apiloom swag.

## When this policy does not apply

If you are reporting a vulnerability in a component of the **Apiloom Platform** (the enterprise product, including hosted services), please still email `security@apiloom.io`. We will route your report to the right team and the response SLAs above apply.

If you are reporting an **abuse incident** on a service operated by Apiloom (rather than a vulnerability), please email `abuse@apiloom.io`.
